Energy check

first law on predicted duties

At steady state, heat in (generator + evaporator) equals heat out (condenser + absorber); the Aspen base case closes to 0.04 %. During transients the gap is the heat being stored in, or released from, the vessel metal and solution. The AI was never told this law, so a small steady-state gap is evidence it learned the physics.

Model comparison

mean NRMSE over 17 outputs · lower is better

All four models were trained on the same 134 runs and scored on the same 34 unseen test runs. A model without memory cannot follow the 18–47 min time constants of the chiller, which is why the static model fails on transients.

Limits of this prototype

The AI reproduces the calibrated MATLAB dynamic model, not measurements from a real chiller. Its accuracy against a real plant is bounded by how well that model, its assumed thermal masses and its ε-NTU heat-transfer correlations, represent the plant.

Predictions are trusted only inside the envelope above. The sliders are limited to it for that reason. Operation with xstrong above 64 wt% (crystallisation) or evaporator temperature below 0 °C (freezing) was excluded from the training data, so the model flags those regions but cannot predict behaviour inside them.

COP_avg30 is computed from the predicted heat duties over a trailing 30 minutes, not fitted separately. Instantaneous COP is not shown because it spikes during transients when generator heat goes into storage.
